How Nether portals link

Every 1 block traveled in the Nether corresponds to 8 blocks in the Overworld along the X and Z axes. Y is unchanged. So an Overworld portal at (800, 70, -400) maps to a Nether target near (100, 70, -50).

The gotcha: search cubes

The game does not teleport you to the exact divided coordinates. Instead, it searches a 128 by 256 by 128 cube in the target dimension centered on those coordinates. Any existing portal inside that cube is a candidate; the game picks the closest one. If none exists, it generates a new portal within the search area, often on a random ledge you did not want.

Best practice

  • Build the Nether portal first. Standing at the divided coordinates in the Nether and lighting a portal there gives you full control over placement. Then travel back through it to place the Overworld side exactly where the game puts you.
  • If you already have the Overworld portal: use these coordinates to enter the Nether at the right location, then build the target portal by hand and light it. The next return trip will link to yours.
  • Spacing: keep paired portals at least 128 blocks apart in the Nether. Two Overworld portals within 1024 blocks of each other risk collapsing to the same Nether portal.
  • Y is preserved. If your Overworld portal is at Y=70, the game will look for or place a Nether portal at Y=70 too. Move the Y target manually to a safe height in the Nether (Y=32 is a common safe floor for the roof-free path).

Roof travel

Y=128 and above in the Nether is empty space, which is why Nether roof highways are popular. If you want to fast-travel long distances, aim your Nether target at roughly the paired X and Z but at Y=128, and use ice/boat or elytra on the roof. This tool leaves Y as-is, so adjust it once you have the horizontal target.
